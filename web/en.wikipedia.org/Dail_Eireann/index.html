Dail Eireann

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dáil Éireann (English: Assembly of Ireland) is the lower house of the Oireachtas, the legislature of Ireland. It is elected directly by universal suffrage under the single transferable vote system of proportional representation. Its members, known as Teachtaí Dála (TDs), are elected from multi-seat constituencies. The Dáil meets at Leinster House in Dublin.

Powers

The Dáil is by far the more powerful of the two houses of the Oireachtas. It nominates the Taoiseach (prime minister), who is then formally appointed by the President. Money bills originate in the Dáil, and the Seanad (upper house) can only recommend amendments and delay legislation. The government must retain the confidence of the Dáil to remain in office. Under the Constitution of Ireland, a general election must take place at least every five years.

History

The name derives from the revolutionary First Dáil, convened in January 1919 by Sinn Féin deputies elected in the 1918 general election, which issued a declaration of independence and a democratic programme. The present institution descends from the Dáil of the Irish Free State (1922), and its constitutional position was settled by the 1937 Constitution.

Composition and officers

Following the 2024 general election, the Dáil has 174 TDs, an increase from 158 under boundary revisions. Its presiding officer is the Ceann Comhairle, elected by secret ballot at the first sitting after a general election. Business is ordered through leaders' questions, debates on legislation, and committee scrutiny conducted largely through select committees mirroring government departments.
