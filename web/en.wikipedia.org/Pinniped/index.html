Pinniped

Pinnipeds (from Latin pinna "fin" and pes "foot"), commonly known as seals, are a widely distributed and diverse clade of carnivorous, fin-footed, semiaquatic, mostly marine mammals. They comprise the families Otariidae (eared seals and sea lions), Phocidae (earless or true seals), and Odobenidae (the walrus), with 34 extant species.

Anatomy and behaviour

Pinnipeds have streamlined bodies, limbs modified into flippers, and thick blubber or dense fur for insulation. Eared seals can rotate their hind flippers forward to move on land and swim mainly with their foreflippers; phocids swim with their hind flippers and are clumsier ashore; walruses are distinguished by their tusks. Most feed on fish and squid, though leopard seals take penguins and other seals. They haul out on land or ice to rest, moult, and give birth, often in large colonies, and many species make long migrations.

Evolution and conservation

Pinnipeds evolved from terrestrial carnivorans about 50 million years ago; the Eocene–Oligocene fossil Enaliarctos and the Miocene "walking seal" Puijila document the transition to aquatic life. Many species were hunted intensively for fur, oil, and ivory; several recovered under 20th-century protection, while some — such as the Mediterranean monk seal — remain endangered.
