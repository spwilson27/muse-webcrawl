Kosynierzy

Kosynierzy (singular: kosynier; English: scythemen) were Polish peasant volunteers armed with war scythes, best known for their role in the Kościuszko Uprising of 1794 against Russia and Prussia.

History

Recruited largely from the peasantry, kosynierzy fought with scythes straightened into glaive-like weapons. Their most famous action was at the Battle of Racławice on 4 April 1794, where a scythe charge helped capture Russian artillery. Bartosz Głowacki, who extinguished a cannon fuse with his cap, became a folk hero of the movement.

Scythe-armed units reappeared in later Polish uprisings, including the November Uprising (1830–31), the Kraków Uprising (1846) — where Edward Dembowski led a procession of scythemen — and the January Uprising (1863–64). The scythemen became an enduring symbol in Polish art and literature, notably in paintings by Jan Matejko and the panorama of Racławice.
