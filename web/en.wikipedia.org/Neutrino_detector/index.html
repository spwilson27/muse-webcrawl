Neutrino detector

A neutrino detector is a physics apparatus designed to study neutrinos — elementary particles that interact only via the weak interaction and gravity, and therefore pass through ordinary matter almost undisturbed. Because neutrino interactions are so rare, detectors must be very large and heavily shielded from background radiation, and are often built deep underground, under water or under ice.

Detection methods

The principal techniques include water Cherenkov detectors, in which a neutrino interaction produces a charged particle that emits Cherenkov radiation detected by photomultiplier tubes; liquid scintillator detectors; radiochemical detectors such as the Homestake experiment, which first measured solar neutrinos; and tracking calorimeters and ice Cherenkov arrays such as IceCube at the South Pole.

Notable detectors

Super-Kamiokande in Japan, the Sudbury Neutrino Observatory in Canada, Borexino in Italy, KamLAND, and IceCube have measured solar, atmospheric, reactor, geoneutrinos and high-energy astrophysical neutrinos, and established the phenomenon of neutrino oscillation.
