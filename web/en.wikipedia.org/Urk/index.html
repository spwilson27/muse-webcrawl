Urk is a municipality and village in the province of Flevoland in the Netherlands. It was, until 1939, an island in the Zuiderzee; the completion of the Noordoostpolder reclamation in 1942 joined it to the mainland. With a population of roughly 21,000, Urk is one of the most distinctive communities in the Netherlands, renowned for its fishing fleet, its strong religious traditions, and its own dialect.

History

Urk is first mentioned in a donation deed of 966. For centuries the islanders lived from fishing in the Zuiderzee. The closing of the Afsluitdijk in 1932 turned the Zuiderzee into the freshwater IJsselmeer, and land reclamation ended Urk's island status — though the village kept its maritime character. During the Second World War the island was a centre of resistance.

Fishing and community

Urk hosts the largest fishing fleet by capacity in the Netherlands and a major fish auction; many Urkers work in fish processing and maritime trades abroad. The community is known for its deeply rooted orthodox Calvinist faith — it is among the most churchgoing and politically conservative municipalities in the country, with many residents voting for the Reformed Political Party (SGP). Traditional costume is still worn by some older residents, and the local dialect, Urkers, is considered one of the most divergent Dutch dialects, with features preserved from older stages of the language.
