Vukovar massacre
Date: 20 November 1991
Location: Ovčara, near Vukovar, Croatia
Victims: at least 200 prisoners of war and civilians
Context: Croatian War of Independence

The Vukovar massacre, also known as the Vukovar hospital massacre, was the killing of Croatian prisoners of war and civilians by Serb paramilitaries, to whom they had been turned over by the Yugoslav People's Army (JNA), on 20 November 1991, shortly after the capture of Vukovar.

Events

After the city fell, about 300 men were removed from Vukovar hospital, despite expectations they would be evacuated under Red Cross supervision. Around 200–264 of them were taken to a warehouse at the Ovčara farm, beaten, then transported in groups to a prepared site where they were shot and buried in a mass grave.

Aftermath and trials

The Ovčara grave was exhumed in 1996, and 200 bodies were identified. The International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia convicted JNA officers in connection with the killings, and Serbian courts in Belgrade also convicted direct perpetrators. The massacre remains one of the most notorious crimes of the Croatian war.
