Binocular disparity

Binocular disparity refers to the difference in image location of an object seen by the left and right eyes, resulting from the eyes' horizontal separation (parallax). The visual system uses disparity as a primary cue for depth perception (stereopsis).

Geometry

Points on the horopter project to corresponding retinal points with zero disparity. Objects nearer than fixation produce crossed disparity; objects farther produce uncrossed disparity. The magnitude of disparity grows as distance from the horopter increases, until fusion fails and diplopia (double vision) occurs beyond Panum's fusional area.

Neural basis

Disparity-selective neurons in primary visual cortex (V1) and extrastriate areas encode absolute and relative disparity. Fine disparity supports precise depth judgements; coarse disparity supports qualitative depth ordering.

Applications

Stereograms, 3D cinema, virtual reality headsets and stereo microscopes exploit disparity by presenting separate images to each eye. Random-dot stereograms, pioneered by Béla Julesz, demonstrate that disparity alone suffices for depth.
