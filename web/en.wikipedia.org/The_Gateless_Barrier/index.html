The Gateless Barrier

The Gateless Barrier (Mandarin: Wumenguan; Japanese: Mumonkan), sometimes translated as The Gateless Gate, is a collection of 48 Chan (Zen) koans compiled in 1228 by the Chinese monk Wumen Huikai (1183–1260). Alongside the Blue Cliff Record, it is one of the most famous collections of koans in Zen Buddhism, and a central text of the Rinzai school in Japan.

Contents and structure

Each case presents a short anecdote or dialogue — a "public case" (gong'an) — drawn from earlier Chan records of encounters between masters and students, followed by Wumen's commentary in prose and a verse summary. Wumen is said to have worked six years on the first case himself: Zhaozhou's "Mu" — a monk asked Zhaozhou whether a dog has Buddha-nature, and Zhaozhou answered "Mu" (無, "no/not").

Title

The title plays on the author's name: Wumen means "no gate" or "gateless", and the collection is a "barrier" (guan, a checkpoint or frontier pass) that blocks the conceptual mind while having, paradoxically, no gate at all. The preface exhorts students to arouse "great doubt" and pass through by total exertion.

Influence

The Mumonkan became a standard part of koan curricula. Notable English translations include those of Nyogen Senzaki and Paul Reps (in Zen Flesh, Zen Bones, 1957) and of Robert Aitken. Famous cases include "What is the sound of one hand?", adapted from Hakuin's use of the collection's tradition, "Nansen kills the cat", and "A monk asked Zhaozhou: does a dog have Buddha-nature?".
