Rogers Commission

The Rogers Commission, formally the Presidential Commission on the Space Shuttle Challenger Accident, was a presidential commission formed by U.S. President Ronald Reagan on 3 February 1986 to investigate the Space Shuttle Challenger disaster of 28 January 1986, in which the orbiter broke apart 73 seconds after launch, killing all seven crew members.

Findings

Chaired by former Secretary of State William P. Rogers, the commission included physicist Richard Feynman and astronaut Sally Ride. It concluded that the disaster was caused by the failure of the O-ring seals in the right solid rocket booster, which did not seal properly in cold weather. Feynman's famous televised demonstration, clamping a piece of O-ring material in ice water, showed the rubber lost resilience at low temperatures. The report also criticised NASA's decision-making culture and communication failures. Feynman appended a personal, sharply critical appendix on the agency's safety culture.

Aftermath

The commission's nine recommendations led to a redesign of the boosters and changes in NASA management; the Shuttle programme was grounded for nearly three years before flights resumed in 1988.
