The First Polish Army (Polish: Pierwsza Armia Wojska Polskiego) was an army formation of the Polish Armed Forces in the East during the Second World War, formed in the Soviet Union in 1944 from the Polish I Corps.

Formation

The army grew out of the Polish formations organised under General Zygmunt Berling with Soviet sponsorship after the break between the USSR and the Polish government-in-exile. By the summer of 1944 it fielded several infantry divisions, armoured and artillery brigades, and supporting units.

Operations

The First Polish Army fought alongside the Red Army's 1st Belorussian Front. It took part in the fighting for Warsaw's eastern suburb of Praga in September 1944, the Vistula–Oder Offensive in January 1945, the capture of Warsaw, the breakthrough of the Pomeranian Wall, the fighting for Kolberg (Kołobrzeg), and finally the Battle of Berlin in April–May 1945, where its units were the only non-Soviet formation to participate.

Legacy

After the war the army's formations became part of the armed forces of the Polish People's Republic.
