Sloe

SpeciesPrunus spinosa
FamilyRosaceae
Common nameBlackthorn

The sloe is the fruit of Prunus spinosa, the blackthorn, a species of flowering plant in the rose family Rosaceae. The fruit is a drupe about 10–12 mm in diameter, black with a purple-blue waxy bloom, ripening in autumn. Sloes are thin-fleshed and extremely astringent when raw, with a sharp, sour taste.

Uses

Sloes are best known as the flavouring for sloe gin, made by steeping the pricked fruit in gin with sugar. They are also used in jams, preserves and liqueurs such as the Basque patxaran. In Britain, sloe gin is traditionally made after the first frosts, which soften the fruit.

The plant

Blackthorn is a large deciduous shrub or small tree growing to about 5 metres tall, with blackish bark and dense, stiff, spiny branches. It is native to Europe, western Asia and northwest Africa, and flowers early in spring before the leaves appear.
