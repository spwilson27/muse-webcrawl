Voina (Russian: Война, meaning "War") is a Russian street-art collective known for provocative and politically charged performance art. Founded in 2006–2007 by Oleg Vorotnikov and Natalia Sokol, the group became famous for actions mocking the Russian authorities, the police and state ideology, and gained international fame in the late 2000s and early 2010s.

Notable actions

Voina's most celebrated action, "Dick Captured by the FSB" (June 2010), painted a 65-metre phallus on the Liteyny Bridge in Saint Petersburg so that, when the drawbridge rose opposite the headquarters of the Federal Security Service (FSB), the image stood erect before it. The work won the Innovation Prize, a state-sponsored contemporary art award, in 2011, to the embarrassment of officials. Other actions included staging a mock hanging of "migrant workers and homosexuals" in a supermarket (2008), tipping over police cars in "Palace Revolution" (2010), and kissing policewomen in Moscow metro stations (2011).

Members and offshoots

Members included Nadezhda Tolokonnikova and Yekaterina Samutsevich, who left to co-found the feminist punk group Pussy Riot in 2011, and Pyotr Verzilov, later of Pussy Riot and Mediazona. Several members faced criminal charges for hooliganism; Banksy paid bail money for arrested members after the police-car action and auctioned work in their support.
