Association of Research Libraries

The Association of Research Libraries (ARL) is a nonprofit organization of 127 research libraries at comprehensive, research-intensive institutions in the United States and Canada. Founded in 1932, it shares the characteristic that its member institutions are engaged in research and offer doctoral degrees.

History

ARL was founded in 1932 by the directors of several major university libraries who saw a need for a collective voice in Washington, D.C. on federal policy affecting research libraries. Its offices are in Washington, D.C.

Activities

ARL works in areas including scholarly communication, public policy, research and access services, and diversity in the research library workforce. It publishes ARL Statistics, an annual compilation of data on the collections, expenditures, staffing, and services of its member libraries, a series dating back to the early 20th century through the work of James Gerould.

Membership

Membership is by invitation and is based on the quality and research intensity of the institution's library. ARL members include most of the largest university libraries in North America, along with the Library of Congress, the National Library of Medicine, and the New York Public Library.
