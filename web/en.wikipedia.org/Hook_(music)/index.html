Hook (music)

HookField: popular music
Definition: a short, catchy musical idea designed to capture the listener's attention
Related: riff, chorus, earworm

A hook is a musical idea — often a short riff, passage or phrase — used in popular music to make a song appealing and to "catch the ear of the listener". The hook is usually found in or consists of the chorus and may be melodic, rhythmic, lyrical or timbral.

Characteristics

Hooks are typically one to four bars long, repeated throughout a song, and engineered for memorability: a distinctive vocal line ("hook line"), a signature instrumental riff, or a striking sound. Commercial songwriting treats hooks as essential, and in the streaming era producers often place a hook within the first seconds of a track to prevent skipping.

Analysis

Musicologists note that a hook need not be the chorus itself; intros, riffs and even production effects can serve as hooks. The related concept of the "earworm" describes a hook that becomes involuntarily memorable. In hip hop, the hook commonly denotes the sung or chanted chorus between verses.
