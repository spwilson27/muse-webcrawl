Capital in economics refers to resources that can enhance one's power to perform economically useful work. In classical economics, capital is one of the three factors of production, alongside land and labour. It includes durable goods created for use in producing other goods and services, such as machinery, tools, buildings, and infrastructure.

History of the concept

The word derives from the Latin capitalis, from caput ("head"). Adam Smith distinguished between fixed capital, which is not consumed in production, and circulating capital, which changes form and is consumed. Karl Marx treated capital not merely as a stock of goods but as a social relation of production.

Types of capital

Economists distinguish physical capital (produced means of production), human capital (skills and knowledge embodied in people), financial capital (funds available for investment), and, in broader usage, natural and social capital. In accounting, capital is the owners' claim on assets after liabilities are deducted.

Capital accumulation

Investment adds to the capital stock while depreciation reduces it. Capital accumulation is a central driver of long-run economic growth in models from the classical economists to the Solow growth model.
