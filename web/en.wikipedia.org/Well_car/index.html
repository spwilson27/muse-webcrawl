A well car, also known as a double-stack car (or also intermodal car / container car), is a type of railroad car specially designed to carry intermodal containers. The "well" is a depressed section which sits close to the rails between the wheel trucks (bogies) of the car, allowing a container to be carried lower than on a traditional flatcar. This lower profile also reduces the risk of loads tipping in high winds and reduces the height of the centre of gravity.

Double-stack service

The chief benefit of the well is that it allows two containers to be stacked, one on top of the other — double-stack service — nearly doubling the capacity of a train without exceeding loading-gauge height limits in North America. Double-stack container service was introduced in North America in the 1980s and became the standard for intermodal freight there, greatly improving the economics of rail container transport.

Design

Well cars are usually built as articulated multi-unit sets sharing bogies between units, in well lengths of 40, 48, or 53 feet to match standard container sizes. The upper container is secured to the lower one with inter-box connectors. Outside North America, low-floor wagon designs such as pocket wagons serve a similar purpose under tighter loading gauges.
