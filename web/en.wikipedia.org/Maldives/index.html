The Maldives, officially the Republic of Maldives, is an archipelagic country in South Asia in the Indian Ocean, southwest of Sri Lanka and India. It consists of 26 coral atolls comprising about 1,200 islands, and is the world's lowest and flattest country, with an average ground level of about 1.5 metres above sea level.

History

The islands were settled over 2,500 years ago and were ruled as an Islamic sultanate from the 12th century. The Maldives became a British protectorate in 1887 and gained independence in 1965, becoming a republic in 1968.

Economy

Tourism is the largest industry, drawing visitors to resort islands and renowned diving and snorkelling sites; fishing, especially tuna, is also important. Climate change and sea-level rise pose a serious threat to the country, prompting national advocacy on global climate policy.
