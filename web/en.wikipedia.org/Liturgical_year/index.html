Liturgical year

Liturgical year
Also: church year, Christian year
Begins: First Sunday of Advent (Western); 1 September (Byzantine)

The liturgical year, also known as the church year, consists of the cycle of liturgical seasons in Christian churches that determines when feast days, including celebrations of saints, are to be observed, and which portions of Scripture are to be read at worship.

Western cycle

In Roman Catholic, Lutheran, Anglican and many other Western churches, the year begins on the First Sunday of Advent, followed by the Christmas season, Ordinary Time, Lent (beginning on Ash Wednesday, including Holy Week), the Easter Triduum, the Easter season (ending at Pentecost), and the long second period of Ordinary Time until Advent returns. Liturgical colours — purple, white, red and green — mark the seasons.

Eastern cycle

The Byzantine Rite year begins on 1 September and is structured around fixed feasts and the movable Paschal cycle, with major fasting periods including Great Lent, the Apostles' Fast, the Dormition Fast and the Nativity Fast.

Lectionaries

Many churches follow lectionaries such as the Revised Common Lectionary, a three-year cycle of readings (Years A, B, C) shared by numerous denominations.
