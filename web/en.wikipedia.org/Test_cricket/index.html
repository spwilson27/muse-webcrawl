Test cricket

A Test match between England and Australia

Test cricket is the longest format of the sport of cricket, regarded as the game's highest standard. A Test match is played over a maximum of five days between national representative teams granted Test status by the International Cricket Council (ICC).

History

The first officially recognized Test match took place on 15–19 March 1877 between Australia and England at the Melbourne Cricket Ground, which Australia won by 45 runs. Test status was gradually extended: South Africa (1889), the West Indies (1928), New Zealand (1930), India (1932), Pakistan (1952), Sri Lanka (1982), Zimbabwe (1992), Bangladesh (2000), and, in 2018, Ireland and Afghanistan—twelve teams in total. The Ashes series between England and Australia, begun in 1882, remains the format's most storied rivalry.

Format

Tests are played with two innings per side. Play runs about six hours a day, with a minimum of 90 overs per day, using a red ball (or pink for day/night Tests). Matches can be won, lost, drawn (time expires with the match incomplete), or tied (of more than 2,400 Tests, only two have ended in ties). Since 2019 the leading teams have contested the ICC World Test Championship, decided by a final between the top two sides in a league table.
