Stanford University (officially the Leland Stanford Junior University) is a private research university in Stanford, California, United States. Founded in 1885 by railroad magnate Leland Stanford and his wife Jane in memory of their only child, Leland Stanford Jr., who had died of typhoid fever at fifteen, it opened its doors in 1891. Stanford is consistently ranked among the world's top universities and is closely associated with the technology industry of nearby Silicon Valley.

Campus and organisation

The campus, among the largest in the United States at 8,180 acres, is organised around the sandstone Main Quad and Memorial Church, in California Mission Revival style. The university comprises seven schools — including Engineering, Medicine, Law, Business and Humanities and Sciences — and research institutes such as SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory and the Hoover Institution. Stanford's faculty and alumni include dozens of Nobel laureates and Turing Award winners.

Silicon Valley

Stanford played a formative role in the development of Silicon Valley: Dean of Engineering Frederick Terman encouraged graduates to found companies locally, a policy that helped give rise to Hewlett-Packard and, through Stanford's industrial parks and licensing, to firms including Google, Yahoo!, Cisco, Netflix and Nike, whose founders studied there.

Admissions and athletics

Admission is among the most selective in the United States, with an acceptance rate around 4 per cent. The Stanford Cardinal compete in NCAA Division I sports and the university's athletes have won medals at every modern Olympic Games; its football rivalry with the University of California, Berkeley is contested in the annual Big Game.
