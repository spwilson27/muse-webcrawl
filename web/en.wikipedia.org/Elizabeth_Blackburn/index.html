Elizabeth Blackburn

Elizabeth Helen Blackburn (born 26 November 1948) is an Australian-American biological researcher who shared the 2009 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Carol W. Greider and Jack W. Szostak "for the discovery of how chromosomes are protected by telomeres and the enzyme telomerase".

Education and early research

Blackburn was born in Hobart, Tasmania, and studied biochemistry at the University of Melbourne before completing her PhD at the University of Cambridge in 1975, working in Frederick Sanger's laboratory on sequencing nucleic acids. As a postdoctoral researcher at Yale University with Joseph Gall, she sequenced the ends of the linear rDNA molecules of the ciliate Tetrahymena, identifying the repeated DNA sequence that forms the telomere.

Discovery of telomerase

Moving to the University of California, Berkeley, Blackburn and her graduate student Carol Greider reported in 1985 the existence of an enzymatic activity — telomerase — that synthesises telomeric repeat DNA. Their work explained how chromosome ends are maintained despite the end-replication problem, with major implications for ageing and cancer biology. From 1990 Blackburn worked at the University of California, San Francisco.

Later career

Blackburn co-authored The Telomere Effect (2017), on telomeres and lifestyle. She served as president of the Salk Institute for Biological Studies from 2016 to 2017. She has also been prominent in debates on bioethics and the treatment of women in science.
