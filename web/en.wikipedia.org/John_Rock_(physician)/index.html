John Rock (physician)

John Charles Rock (March 24, 1890 – December 4, 1984) was an American obstetrician and gynecologist best known for his role, alongside biologist Gregory Pincus, in the development of the first effective oral contraceptive, the birth control pill.

Career

Rock was educated at Harvard Medical School and spent much of his career at Harvard and at the Free Hospital for Women in Brookline, Massachusetts, where he directed a fertility clinic. Ironically, his early research aimed at helping infertile couples conceive; studying how progesterone suppressed ovulation, he collaborated with Pincus on trials of synthetic progestins, including trials conducted in Puerto Rico in the 1950s. The pill Enovid was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for contraceptive use in 1960.

Later life

A devout Catholic, Rock campaigned to persuade the Roman Catholic Church to accept the pill, arguing in his 1963 book The Time Has Come: A Catholic Doctor's Proposals to End the Battle over Birth Control that it worked with natural processes. The Church rejected his position in the 1968 encyclical Humanae vitae. Rock founded the Rock Reproductive Study Center and remained a leading voice in reproductive medicine until his death at age 94.
