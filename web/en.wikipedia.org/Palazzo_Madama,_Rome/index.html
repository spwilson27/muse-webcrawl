Palazzo Madama, Rome

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Palazzo Madama is a palace in Rome, Italy, which since 1871 has housed the seat of the Senate of the Italian Republic. It stands on Corso del Rinascimento, a short distance west of Piazza Navona, and should not be confused with the Palazzo Madama in Turin.

History

The palace was built on land belonging to the Medici family, over and beside the ruins of the ancient Baths of Nero. It took its name from "Madama" Margaret of Austria, the illegitimate daughter of Emperor Charles V and wife first of Alessandro de' Medici and then of Ottavio Farnese, who lived there in the sixteenth century. The palace later passed to the Medici grand dukes of Tuscany and was enlarged over successive centuries.

The present Baroque facade, completed in the mid-seventeenth century by Paolo Marucelli and others, is richly decorated with carved ornament. Inside, the palace contains frescoed state rooms, a celebrated library, and the Senate chamber, which was adapted from the palace's former courtyard and stables areas after the building became the seat of the Senate of the Kingdom of Italy in 1871.

The Senate seat

Following the capture of Rome in 1870 and the transfer of the capital from Florence, Palazzo Madama was assigned to the Senate. The first sitting took place on 28 November 1871. After the fall of the monarchy, it continued as the seat of the Senate of the Republic under the 1948 constitution. The building is protected and visits are by guided tour when the Senate is not sitting.
