Novi Pazar (Serbian Cyrillic: Нови Пазар) is a city in southwestern Serbia, in the Raška District, and the cultural centre of the historical Sandžak region and of Serbia's Bosniak community. Its name means "new marketplace" in both Serbian and Turkish (Yeni Pazar).

History

The town was founded in the 1460s by the Ottoman general Isa-beg Ishaković near the medieval Serbian capital of Ras, whose early churches — including the 9th-century Church of Saints Peter and Paul, among the oldest in Serbia — and the nearby Sopoćani monastery (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) reflect the area's importance to the medieval Serbian state. Under Ottoman rule Novi Pazar grew into a prosperous trading town on the route between Sarajevo and Skopje, with caravanserais, mosques, and a fortress. It was the seat of the Ottoman Sanjak of Novi Pazar, whose status was disputed at the Congress of Berlin in 1878, and passed to Serbia in 1912 after the First Balkan War.

Demographics and culture

Bosniaks form the large majority of the population, and the city is the seat of Bosniak cultural and Islamic institutions in Serbia, including one of the country's Islamic communities and the International University of Novi Pazar. The Altun-Alem Mosque and the old bazaar preserve the Ottoman urban character.
