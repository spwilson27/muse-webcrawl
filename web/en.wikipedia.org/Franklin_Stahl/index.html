Franklin Stahl

Franklin W. Stahl (born 1929), geneticist of the Meselson–Stahl experiment

Franklin William Stahl (born October 8, 1929) is an American molecular biologist and geneticist. With Matthew Meselson, he conducted the celebrated Meselson–Stahl experiment (1958), which demonstrated that DNA replication is semiconservative — often called "the most beautiful experiment in biology".

The Meselson–Stahl experiment

Working at Caltech, Meselson and Stahl grew E. coli in medium containing the heavy isotope nitrogen-15, then shifted the bacteria to light nitrogen-14 and separated their DNA by density-gradient centrifugation in caesium chloride. After one generation the DNA had an intermediate density, and after two generations intermediate and light bands appeared in equal amounts — exactly the pattern predicted if each daughter double helix keeps one parental strand, refuting conservative replication and supporting the Watson–Crick model.

Later career

Stahl became a professor at the University of Oregon, where he worked on genetic recombination, especially in bacteriophage lambda, helping establish how crossing over occurs at the molecular level and developing the study of recombination "hotspots" such as the Chi site. He was elected to the National Academy of Sciences, received a MacArthur "genius" fellowship, and mentored generations of geneticists; he is professor emeritus at Oregon's Institute of Molecular Biology.
