Berlin Trilogy

The Berlin Trilogy is a set of three studio albums by the English musician David Bowie, recorded in collaboration with the producer Tony Visconti and the musician Brian Eno: Low (1977), "Heroes" (1977), and Lodger (1979). Named for Bowie's residence in West Berlin during the period, the albums combined rock songs with experimental, largely instrumental and ambient pieces influenced by German electronic and krautrock acts such as Kraftwerk and Neu!.

Background

Bowie moved to West Berlin in 1976, sharing an apartment in Schöneberg with Iggy Pop, in an effort to escape a cocaine addiction that had taken hold during his time in Los Angeles. The city's atmosphere — divided, cheap, and home to a vibrant underground art and music scene — shaped the fractured, atmospheric character of the records.

The albums

Low, recorded mostly in France, split between conventional songs and a second side of moody instrumental pieces with titles evoking Warsaw and Berlin. "Heroes", recorded at Hansa Tonstudio near the Berlin Wall, included the title track, whose lyric of lovers by the Wall became one of Bowie's signature songs. Lodger, recorded in Switzerland and New York, dropped the instrumentals for a set of restless songs partly inspired by world music and travel.

Legacy

Initially received cautiously, the trilogy came to be regarded as among Bowie's finest work, exerting a major influence on post-punk, new wave, and electronic music.
