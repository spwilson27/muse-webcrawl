Universal joint

A simple universal joint (Cardan joint) connecting two rotating shafts.

TypeMechanical coupling
Also calledCardan joint, U-joint, Hardy Spicer joint
Used inDrive shafts, steering columns, machinery

A universal joint (also called a Cardan joint or U-joint) is a joint or coupling in a rigid rod that allows the rod to bend in any direction, and is commonly used in shafts that transmit rotary motion. It consists of a pair of hinges located close together, oriented at 90° to each other, connected by a cross shaft. The device is named after Gerolamo Cardano, who described it in the 16th century, though gimbal mechanisms of this kind were known much earlier.

Mechanics

A single universal joint does not transmit motion at a constant velocity ratio: when the input shaft rotates uniformly, the output shaft speeds up and slows down twice per revolution, with the fluctuation increasing with the angle between the shafts. For this reason, universal joints in vehicle drive shafts are usually used in pairs, arranged so that the fluctuations cancel, or replaced by constant-velocity joints where smooth transmission is essential.

History

The basic concept rests on gimbals, known since antiquity. Cardano described a related arrangement in De subtilitate (1550), and Robert Hooke analysed the non-uniform motion and built joints for astronomical instruments in the 17th century. In the 20th century the joint became a standard component of automobile propeller shafts, patented forms being produced by companies such as Spicer.

Applications

Universal joints are used in automotive drive shafts, steering columns, agricultural machinery power take-offs, rolling mills, and hand tools such as socket wrenches. Double Cardan joints and centering mechanisms reduce speed fluctuation in compact installations.
