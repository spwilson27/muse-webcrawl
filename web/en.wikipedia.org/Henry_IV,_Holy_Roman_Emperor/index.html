Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor

Henry IV (German: Heinrich IV; 11 November 1050 – 7 August 1106) was Holy Roman Emperor from 1084 to 1105, King in Germany from 1054 to 1105, King of Italy and Burgundy from 1056 to 1105, and Duke of Bavaria from 1052 to 1054. He was the son of Henry III, Holy Roman Emperor — the second monarch of the Salian dynasty — and Agnes of Poitou.

Early life and minority

After his father's death in 1056, Henry was placed under the regency of his mother. Archbishop Anno II of Cologne seized the young king in the Coup of Kaiserswerth in 1062, and the regency passed through rival prelates. Henry took personal rule in 1065.

Investiture Controversy

Henry's conflict with Pope Gregory VII over the right to invest bishops — the Investiture Controversy — defined his reign. In 1076 Gregory excommunicated and deposed him; German princes elected an anti-king, Rudolf of Rheinfelden. In January 1077 Henry made a penitential journey to Canossa Castle, standing in the snow to beg absolution — the famous Walk to Canossa. Absolved, he later fought Rudolf, marched on Rome, installed Antipope Clement III, and was crowned emperor by him in 1084. Gregory died in exile in 1085.

Later reign and death

Henry faced rebellions by his sons Conrad (1093) and Henry (1105). Forced to abdicate in 1105, he escaped captivity but died in Liège in 1106. His body was eventually buried in Speyer Cathedral after the excommunication was lifted posthumously.
