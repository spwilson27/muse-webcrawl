Andrea Palladio

Born: 30 November 1508, Padua, Republic of Venice

Died: 19 August 1580, Maser, Republic of Venice

Occupation: Architect

Movement: Renaissance, Palladianism

Andrea Palladio (1508–1580) was an Italian Renaissance architect of the Republic of Venice. Inspired by Roman and Greek classical architecture and guided by his patron Gian Giorgio Trissino, Palladio became the most influential architect of country houses and villas in the Veneto. His treatise I quattro libri dell'architettura (The Four Books of Architecture, 1570) spread his system of harmonious proportions across Europe and America, giving rise to Palladianism.

Life and works

Born Andrea di Pietro della Gondola in Padua, he trained as a stonemason before Trissino renamed him "Palladio", after the Greek goddess Pallas Athene's epithet. His celebrated villas — the Villa Rotonda (La Rotonda), Villa Barbaro and Villa Emo — combine temple-front porticoes with working farm buildings arranged symmetrically. In Vicenza he built the Basilica Palladiana, the Teatro Olimpico and numerous palazzi; in Venice, the churches of San Giorgio Maggiore and Il Redentore.

Legacy

Palladio's influence passed through Inigo Jones to English country houses, and through pattern books to the architecture of colonial and early republican America, notably Thomas Jefferson's Monticello and the University of Virginia. The City of Vicenza and the Palladian Villas of the Veneto are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the term "Palladian window" preserves his name in everyday architectural vocabulary.
