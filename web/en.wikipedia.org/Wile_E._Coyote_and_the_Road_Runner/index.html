Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner

Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner are cartoon characters from the Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies series of animated cartoons, created by animation director Chuck Jones and writer Michael Maltese in 1948. In each cartoon, the Coyote repeatedly attempts to catch and eat the Road Runner, a fast-running ground bird, using elaborate contraptions purchased from the fictional Acme Corporation — which invariably backfire.

Format and rules

The cartoons are set in the deserts of the American Southwest, rendered in a stylised, painterly manner. Chuck Jones enumerated a set of humorous "rules" for the series: the Road Runner never leaves the road, never speaks except to say "Beep, beep!," the Coyote is never harmed by anything other than his own devices and gravity, and no outside force can harm the Coyote — only his own ineptitude or the failure of Acme products. The Coyote almost never catches the Road Runner, and communicates with the audience through held-up signs and expressions of exasperation.

History and legacy

The pair debuted in Fast and Furry-ous (1949) and starred in dozens of theatrical shorts, most directed by Jones in the 1950s and 1960s. The characters became enduring icons of Warner Bros. animation; the Coyote was granted a speaking role in a few encounters with Bugs Bunny, and a feature film, Coyote vs. Acme, was produced (and then shelved by Warner Bros. Discovery) in the 2020s, becoming a widely discussed case in entertainment industry news.
