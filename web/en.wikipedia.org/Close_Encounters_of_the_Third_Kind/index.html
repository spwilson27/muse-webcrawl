Close Encounters of the Third Kind

Close Encounters of the Third Kind is a 1977 American science fiction drama film written and directed by Steven Spielberg and starring Richard Dreyfuss, Melinda Dillon, Teri Garr and François Truffaut. It tells the story of Roy Neary, an everyday worker in Indiana, whose encounter with an unidentified flying object leads him on an obsessive quest that converges with a government team's first contact with extraterrestrial visitors.

Plot

After a close encounter leaves him with a sunburn-like mark and an inexplicable mental image of a mountain shape, Roy becomes obsessed, eventually recognising the shape as Devils Tower in Wyoming. Meanwhile, scientist Claude Lacombe (Truffaut), interpreting ufologist J. Allen Hynek's classification, prepares a secret rendezvous. At the landing site, the mothership communicates with humans through a sequence of musical tones before Roy joins the visitors aboard.

Production and release

The title refers to Hynek's scale, in which a "close encounter of the third kind" means contact with animate beings. Douglas Trumbull supervised the visual effects, and John Williams composed the score, including the famous five-note motif used as a language. The film was a major critical and commercial success, winning Academy Awards for cinematography and sound effects editing; a "Special Edition" followed in 1980 and a Director's Cut in 1998.

Legacy

The film helped legitimise UFO subject matter in mainstream cinema and is preserved in the U.S. National Film Registry for its cultural significance.
