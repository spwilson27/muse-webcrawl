Three Secrets of Fátima

The Three Secrets of Fátima are a series of apocalyptic visions and prophecies which were given to three young Portuguese shepherds, Lúcia Santos and her cousins Jacinta and Francisco Marto, by a Marian apparition, starting on 13 May 1917. According to the children, the apparition — later known as Our Lady of Fátima — imparted three secrets during the apparition of 13 July 1917.

The secrets

The first secret was a vision of Hell. The second included a prediction of the end of the First World War and the coming of a worse war, and a request for the consecration of Russia. The third secret, written down by Sister Lúcia in 1944 and kept sealed by the Vatican for decades, was released in 2000; it described, in symbolic imagery, a "bishop in white" being killed amid persecution of the Church. The Vatican interpreted it as referring to the sufferings of the Church in the twentieth century and the 1981 assassination attempt on Pope John Paul II.

Controversy

The long secrecy surrounding the third part gave rise to extensive speculation and conspiracy theories, and some critics have questioned whether the text published in 2000 was complete. The events at Fátima remain among the most widely known Marian apparitions recognized by the Catholic Church.
