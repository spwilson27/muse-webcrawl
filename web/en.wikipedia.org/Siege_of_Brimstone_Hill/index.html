Siege of Brimstone Hill

The siege of Brimstone Hill was a French siege of the British fortress on Brimstone Hill, Saint Kitts, in the West Indies during the American Revolutionary War. The siege lasted from 19 January to 12 February 1782 and ended with the surrender of the British garrison, giving France control of Saint Kitts.

Background

France had entered the American Revolutionary War as an ally of the United States and sought to capture British sugar islands in the Caribbean. In January 1782, a French expedition under the Marquis de Bouillé landed on Saint Kitts, supported by the fleet of the Comte de Grasse. The British garrison withdrew to the formidable Brimstone Hill Fortress, sometimes called the "Gibraltar of the West Indies."

The siege

De Bouillé invested the fortress and hauled artillery up to bombard it. A British relief attempt by Admiral Samuel Hood, who seized the anchorage at Basseterre in a bold manoeuvre, could not relieve the fortress. After nearly a month of bombardment that badly damaged the fortifications, the governor, Thomas Fraser, surrendered on 12 February 1782 on honourable terms; the garrison was allowed to march out with the honours of war.

Aftermath

Saint Kitts and Nevis were returned to Britain under the Treaty of Paris (1783). Brimstone Hill Fortress was further expanded by the British afterwards and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
