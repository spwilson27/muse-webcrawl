M.M.Warburg & CO (AG & Co.) KGaA is a German private bank and banking group based in Hamburg. Founded in 1798 by Moses Marcus Warburg, it is one of Germany's oldest private banks and remains closely associated with the Warburg family of bankers and scholars.

History

The bank began as a money-changing and lending house in Altona and Hamburg and grew during the nineteenth century into an internationally connected merchant bank, financing trade and issuing bonds. Under Max M. Warburg (director from the 1890s until 1938), it became one of the most important private banks in the German Empire; Max Warburg advised the German delegation at Versailles and sat on the board of the Reichsbank.

Under Nazi persecution the Jewish-owned bank was forcibly "Aryanised" in 1938 and renamed Brinckmann, Wirtz & Co. After the Second World War, Eric M. Warburg — who had built the family's American banking connections — returned to Germany and recovered the firm, which resumed the Warburg name in 1969.

Today

The Warburg Group provides private banking, asset management, and corporate finance services, with offices across Germany. The family name is also associated with other institutions founded by Warburg relatives abroad, including Kuhn, Loeb & Co. in New York and S. G. Warburg & Co. in London, as well as the art historian Aby Warburg, founder of the Warburg Institute.
