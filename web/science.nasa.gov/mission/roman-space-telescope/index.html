Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope

NASA's next great observatory, Roman will image enormous swaths of sky in infrared light — a field of view 100 times larger than Hubble's infrared vision at the same resolution — to hunt exoplanets and measure the effects of dark energy and dark matter.

About the Mission

The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope (formerly WFIRST) is a NASA observatory designed to settle essential questions in dark energy, exoplanets and infrared astrophysics. Like Hubble it has a 2.4-metre primary mirror, but its Wide Field Instrument gives it a field of view roughly 100 times Hubble's infrared instruments, letting it survey the sky thousands of times faster. Its Coronagraph Instrument is a technology demonstration for directly imaging exoplanets — blocking a star's glare to reveal faint planets beside it, a rehearsal for future missions that aim to photograph Earth-like worlds.

Roman's microlensing survey toward the galactic bulge is expected to discover thousands of exoplanets, including some of very low mass, completing the statistical census of planetary systems begun by Kepler. The telescope is named for Nancy Grace Roman, NASA's first chief astronomer and the “Mother of Hubble”. It is being built and managed by NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center, with contributions from JPL, the Space Telescope Science Institute, and international partners including ESA. Launch is planned on a SpaceX Falcon Heavy no later than May 2027, with teams working toward readiness as early as autumn 2026, to an orbit at L2 alongside Webb.

Fast Facts

StatusIn development; launch no later than May 2027 (targeting as early as fall 2026)
Launch vehicleSpaceX Falcon Heavy, Kennedy Space Center
OrbitHalo orbit around Sun–Earth L2, ~1.5 million km from Earth
Primary mirror2.4 metres — the same size as Hubble's
