Psyche

The first mission to a metal asteroid: Psyche is travelling to an iron-nickel-rich world in the main asteroid belt that may be the exposed core of an ancient planetesimal — a building block of the rocky planets.

About the Mission

Psyche launched on 13 October 2023 aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy from Kennedy Space Center. Propelled mainly by solar-electric (Hall-effect) thrusters, and with a Mars gravity assist planned for 2026, it will reach the asteroid Psyche in 2029 and orbit it for about 26 months, descending through a series of orbits to map its surface, gravity field and magnetic field.

The asteroid Psyche, discovered in 1852, is about 226 kilometres (140 miles) across at its widest and orbits the Sun between Mars and Jupiter. If it is indeed the stripped metal core of a failed planet, studying it is the only way to examine a planetary core up close — Earth's own core is forever out of reach. The mission is led by Arizona State University (principal investigator Lindy Elkins-Tanton), managed by JPL, and built on a Maxar chassis. Riding along is the DSOC experiment, which has demonstrated laser (optical) communications from deep space at record distances.

Fast Facts

Launch13 October 2023, Falcon Heavy, Kennedy Space Center
Arrival at asteroid2029; ~26 months in orbit
TargetAsteroid 16 Psyche, ~226 km wide, main belt between Mars and Jupiter
PropulsionSolar-electric Hall thrusters (plus chemical system)
InstrumentsMultispectral imager, gamma-ray and neutron spectrometer, magnetometer, X-band radio science
