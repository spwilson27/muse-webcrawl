Europa Clipper

NASA's largest planetary spacecraft is on its way to Jupiter to explore Europa — an icy moon with a global salty ocean beneath its shell, and one of the most promising places to search for habitable conditions.

About the Mission

Europa Clipper launched on 14 October 2024 aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy from Kennedy Space Center in Florida. It will fly past Mars (2025) and Earth (2026) for gravity assists and arrive at Jupiter in April 2030. Rather than orbiting Europa itself — which would expose it to intense radiation — the spacecraft will orbit Jupiter and make about 49 close flybys of Europa, some passing within roughly 25 kilometres of the surface, building up a near-global map with cameras, spectrometers, an ice-penetrating radar and a magnetometer.

The mission's goal is not to detect life directly, but to determine whether the ocean under Europa's ice — thought to hold roughly twice the volume of Earth's oceans — together with the moon's chemistry and geology, could support life. The spacecraft was assembled and is managed by NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL), with the Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory as a key partner. It carries a metal vault plate bearing a poem, microchip names submitted by the public, and a recording of the word for “water” spoken in many languages.

Fast Facts

Launch14 October 2024, Falcon Heavy, Kennedy Space Center
Arrival at JupiterApril 2030
JourneyAbout 2.9 billion km (1.8 billion miles), with Mars and Earth gravity assists
Tour~49 Europa flybys while orbiting Jupiter; closest passes ~25 km altitude
SizeSolar arrays span about 30.5 metres tip to tip — wider than a basketball court is long
InstrumentsNine science instruments, including ice-penetrating radar (REASON) and a magnetometer
Lead centreJet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL), with APL
