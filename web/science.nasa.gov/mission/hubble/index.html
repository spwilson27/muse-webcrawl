Hubble Space Telescope

Launched in 1990, Hubble has transformed our understanding of the universe, taking more than a million observations and peering back across most of cosmic time.

About the Mission

The Hubble Space Telescope was deployed from the space shuttle Discovery on 25 April 1990 into low Earth orbit, roughly 535 kilometres up. Soon after launch a flaw — spherical aberration — was found in its 2.4-metre primary mirror; astronauts repaired Hubble on the first servicing mission in 1993, installing corrective optics, and four further shuttle servicing missions (the last in 2009) replaced instruments and gyroscopes and kept the observatory at the cutting edge for more than three decades.

Hubble observations have pinned down the age and expansion rate of the universe, shown that most galaxies harbour central black holes, produced the Hubble Deep Field images crowded with thousands of distant galaxies, and measured the atmospheres of exoplanets. Hubble is a partnership between NASA and ESA and is operated by the Space Telescope Science Institute in Baltimore. It now works in tandem with Webb: Hubble sees chiefly in ultraviolet and visible light, Webb in infrared.

Fast Facts

Launch24 April 1990, space shuttle Discovery (STS-31); deployed 25 April 1990
OrbitLow Earth orbit, ~535 km altitude, one orbit about every 95 minutes
Primary mirror2.4 metres diameter
SizeAbout 13.2 metres long — roughly the length of a large school bus
Wavelength rangeUltraviolet, visible and near-infrared (about 0.1 to 2.5 micrometres)
Servicing missionsFive astronaut servicing missions, 1993–2009
NamesakeAstronomer Edwin Hubble, who showed the universe is expanding
