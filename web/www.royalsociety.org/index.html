The Royal Society

The Royal Society is a Fellowship of many of the world's most eminent scientists and the oldest scientific academy in continuous existence, founded in 1660. Its motto, Nullius in verba — 'take nobody's word for it' — states its founding spirit.

The Fellowship

About 1,700 Fellows and Foreign Members (as recalled) include around 85 Nobel laureates; new Fellows are elected annually in what remains one of science's highest honours. The Society's journals began with Philosophical Transactions in 1665 — the world's first science journal.

What it does

Grants and fellowships for researchers, science policy advice to government, public events, the Summer Science Exhibition each July in London, and prizes and medals including the Copley Medal (the oldest surviving scientific award, as often noted).
