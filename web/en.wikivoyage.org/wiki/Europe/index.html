A travel guide to Europe

Europe is a continent of dense history, varied cultures and easy travel between countries. Wikivoyage's Europe guide divides the continent into regions — the Balkans, Britain and Ireland, Central Europe, France and the Low Countries, Greece and Turkey, Iberia, Italy, Russia and the Caucasus, and Scandinavia and the Nordic countries — and lists its most visited cities, from Paris, London and Rome to Berlin, Madrid, Vienna, Amsterdam and Istanbul (partly in Europe).

[Image: Map of Europe with Wikivoyage's travel regions colour-coded]

Get in

Major air hubs include London, Frankfurt, Paris, Amsterdam, Madrid and Istanbul. Within Europe, an extensive rail network — and passes such as Interrail / Eurail — makes overland travel practical; budget airlines link most cities.

See and do

Historic old towns, cathedrals and castles, world-class museums, Alpine and Mediterranean landscapes, festivals and Christmas markets are among the continent's classic draws.

Practicalities

Most of the European Union and several neighbours form the Schengen Area with border-free travel; the euro is used across much of the continent, alongside currencies such as the pound sterling, Swiss franc and Scandinavian crowns.
