Chili Crisp Everything: The Condiment Los Angeles Can't Stop Putting on Everything

By Los Angeles Times Food Staff  ·  Published for the Food section
Homemade chili crisp, the crunchy condiment of the moment. (Illustration placeholder)

Eggs. Noodles. Pizza. Avocado toast. Ice cream, even. There is no dish in Los Angeles that has not been improved, at some point this year, by a spoonful of chili crisp — the crunchy, savory, moderately spicy oil-based condiment that migrated from Sichuan pantry shelves to the center of the city's plate.

What it is

At its simplest, chili crisp is vegetable oil infused with chili flakes, fried shallots or garlic, fermented soybeans, and often peanuts or sesame. The crunch is the point: it delivers heat plus texture, and it wakes up anything it touches.

Where Angelenos use it

  • Morning eggs — a spoonful over fried eggs with a torn piece of scallion.
  • Noodles — tossed with sesame noodles, bok choy and a splash of black vinegar.
  • Vanilla ice cream — the sweet-heat trick that converted every skeptic at our tasting.

Make it at home

Makes about 1½ cups. Heat 1 cup neutral oil to 300°F. Pour over a heatproof bowl containing ½ cup chili flakes, 3 minced garlic cloves, 2 tablespoons sesame seeds, 1 teaspoon salt and 1 tablespoon sugar. Stir in ¼ cup crushed fried shallots and 2 tablespoons roasted peanuts. Cool, jar, refrigerate up to a month.

Store in the refrigerator and always use a clean spoon.
