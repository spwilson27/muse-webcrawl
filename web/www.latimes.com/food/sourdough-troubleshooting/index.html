Sourdough Troubleshooting: Why Your Loaf Didn't Rise (and How to Fix It)

By Los Angeles Times Food Staff  ·  Published for the Food section
A healthy crumb: irregular holes, glossy walls. (Illustration placeholder)

Every sourdough baker has produced the dreaded brick. The good news: nearly every failure has a name, a cause and a fix. Match your loaf to the table below.

ProblemLikely causeFix
Flat, dense loafWeak or underfed starterFeed the starter twice daily for two days before baking; use it at its peak.
Gummy crumbCut too soon, or underbakedWait at least 2 hours before slicing; bake to a deep mahogany crust.
Sour, slack doughOverproofed in a warm kitchenShorten bulk fermentation; watch the dough (50–75% rise), not the clock.
Pale crustNot enough steam or oven heatBake in a preheated Dutch oven with the lid on for the first 20 minutes.
Tight, uniform holesUnderproofedGive bulk fermentation more time; the dough should feel airy and jiggle.

The one rule that fixes most loaves

Temperature is an ingredient. A dough at 78°F behaves nothing like one at 68°F. Keep notes, change one variable at a time, and your starter will teach you the rest.
