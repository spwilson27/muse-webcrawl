Restaurant Row: Where to Eat on L.A.'s Most Delicious Streets

By Los Angeles Times Food Staff  ·  Published for the Food section
Patio season on one of the city's great eating streets. (Illustration placeholder)

Los Angeles doesn't have one great restaurant street — it has dozens. Our critics mapped the corridors where you can park once and eat brilliantly all night: the noodle shops and dumpling houses of the San Gabriel Valley, the taqueria-dense stretches of the Eastside, the old-school red-sauce avenues of the Westside, and the bakery row that turns weekend mornings into a parade of croissants.

The strategy

Pick a street, not a reservation. The joy of a restaurant row is grazing: a dumpling here, a taco there, something sweet at the end. Start at one end, walk the length, and let the line outside a place be your guide — in this city, the line is rarely wrong.

What to order

On any row, order the dish the place is named for. Soup dumplings at the dumpling house. Birria at the birria truck. Hand-pulled noodles at the noodle counter. Rows reward specialists.

Before you go

Rows get busy on Friday and Saturday nights; weeknights are the locals' secret. Bring cash for the trucks and patience for the waits — both are worth it.

This roundup is updated seasonally as new spots open along the city's eating streets.
